Patching Memories

Narratives of Displacement Across South Africa

Find Out More

Project Description

This project seeks to complement the District Six Museum’s physical space with an interactive digital space. Rather than presenting forced displacement only as a history experienced in one locality, the digital platform will allow users to explore its broader scale while recognizing the individual experiences that make up that history. Digital space provides the freedom to travel across geographical distances in an accessible way, connecting individual stories from different communities and regions. By mapping these experiences, the project will place the history of District Six within a broader history of forced displacement in South Africa while maintaining attention to individual experiences. The project will also create an interactive space for people to contribute to the narrative of forced displacement. It provides an opportunity for communities to contribute their own narratives, including experiences that may not currently be represented in the museum.

Deliverables and Outcomes

The primary outcome will be an interactive website featuring a map of South Africa populated by points representing stories of forced displacement across the country. Users will be able to click on individual points to open a dialogue box containing details about each story. These stories will then be connected thematically to the permanent, present, and past exhibitions of the District Six Museum, highlighting relevant aspects of the museum’s collection. The website will also include a contribution portal where visitors can provide their own narratives of displacement, if they choose to do so. The final website will therefore function not only as a digital map, but also as a participatory space for connecting, contributing, and exploring histories of forced displacement across South Africa. The proposed domain/URL could be: www.districtsix.co.za/patchingmemories, or patchingmemories.co.za.

Data, Information, and Content

The content of this project is tripartite. First, as it is an elaboration upon District Six — demonstrating that narratives of displacement are both hyperlocal and widespread, it will draw on data housed within the permanent archive collections of the museum, emphasizing the sound, photography, and art holdings. Second, it will draw on data gathered in concert with our partners including curated artifacts related to country-wide displacement narratives including oral histories, various texts and images. The project’s third data source will be from user contributions solicited through the project’s contribution portal. We are currently finalizing individual data collection protocols that adhere to rigorous ethical and privacy standards with our university partners, as well as co-designing data management protocols and storage infrastructure. Finally, we are collaborating with our university partners to develop our geographic point database to populate our map. This will be an ongoing process as we continue to receive contributions.

Functionality and Technology

Patching Memories Together will consist of four responsive web pages that meet Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG2) hosted on the District Six Museum’s existing website: the living map, submission portal, team page, and partners page. We will use Bootstrap 5 as our CSS framework, and Leaflet to develop our interactive, dynamic map.

The map echoes the aesthetics of District Six’s permanent exhibit, but will represent the entire country of South Africa. When users click on a point, they will “travel” via an animation (to evoke the dynamism of displacement) to that location. A dialog will pop up displaying the displacement story and links to any thematically related District Six Museum holdings from their collection.

Users will be invited to contribute their own story to the project from the initial pop-up window, as well as from a menu option next to the heading of the map webpage. When they navigate here, they will have two options: first, they can request to be put in contact with project partners to facilitate the submission of their personal story; second, they can fill out a series of dialogs, including name, contact information, optional file upload, and a place to input their narrative. These will be reviewed by District Six Museum staff and project partners before being added to the map. Additionally, the navigation menu on the map page will also link to our team and partner highlight pages. The team page will have pertinent contact information, and the partner page will link to each partner’s external web presence.

Audience

People wishing to learn more about the experience of forced displacement in South Africa.

The project will provide a space where their experiences can be represented, explored, and connected to other narratives of displacement across the country. Also we target people who want to learn what it feels like to experience forced displacement and better understand its broader historical and social impact through personal narratives.

Environmental Scan

There are a number of extant projects that address the subject of forced displacement. The Amplification Project is a digital archive developed around the preservation and sharing of art and other cultural productions relating to forced displacement and being a refugee. Additionally, it provides a model for how a project like this may solicit contributions from individuals. The Displacement Series and The Last Refuge both provide examples of narratives similar to those that Patching Memories seeks to present: the interplay between an individual’s experience and the countrywide (and global) occurrence of displacement. The Urban Displacement Project, Mapping Apartheid Anti-Solidarity Map, and The Happy Map all offer examples around potential user interface. The first is an example of a site and user interface tailored more towards researchers, while the latter two offer up a more polished, engaging, and characterful user experience, which Patching Memories seeks to emulate.
